\section{Limitations}

This study has several limitations.

First, the initial benchmark is limited in size and may not fully represent
the diversity of algorithmic programming problems encountered in technical
interviews or real-world software engineering.

Second, the initial experiment focuses on a single Large Language Model.
Therefore, the results may not generalize to other models with different
architectures, training data, or reasoning capabilities.

Third, prompt engineering strategies are difficult to define objectively.
Different prompt designs may produce different results, and the three
strategies evaluated in this study represent only a subset of possible
prompting approaches.

Fourth, runtime measurements may depend on the execution environment.
Although the evaluation framework attempts to maintain consistent execution
conditions, differences in hardware and system configuration may introduce
measurement variation.

Finally, this study evaluates algorithmic programming tasks rather than the
complete set of skills required by software engineers. Algorithmic problem
solving is only one component of software engineering, and performance on
LeetCode-style problems should not be interpreted as a complete measure of
software engineering ability.
